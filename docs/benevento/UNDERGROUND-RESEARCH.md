# Underground sites and the AD 700 reconstruction

Research scope: the Living Museum in Triggio, San Marco dei Sabariani, and the
cathedral archaeological area. This report records directly retrieved sources;
it does not alter the map or `HISTORY.md`.

## Actionable conclusion

**None of the three modern visitor sites supplies a verified plan for a connected
underground route in AD 700.** Their surviving archaeology is real, but “below
today's street/floor” does not establish “underground when first built.”

* Keep the cathedral's early basilica and atrium at their reconstructed historical
  ground level. Do not copy the modern hypogeum visitor route into AD 700.
* Do not replace the invented Santi Quaranta burial network with the same network
  under Sabariani or Triggio. These sources do not justify that geometry.
* The Triggio address and modern operator map pin are verified below. They can
  anchor a research marker; they do not establish an excavation footprint or
  ancient entrance elevation.
* Sabariani is evidence of local burials and a crypt, but neither an exact AD 700
  chamber plan nor its original access/ground level was established in this pass.
* No link among these sites was found. Keep distinct local structures distinct.

## 1. Living Museum, Via Bosco Lucarelli 13

### Direct operator sources

[Visitor route](https://beneventolongobarda.it/il-living-museum/) and
[location/contact page](https://beneventolongobarda.it/dove-siamo-e-contatti/).

The contact page gives the address explicitly. Its HTML map component contains
`data-map-latitute="41.1297136" data-map-longitude="14.7746071"` (the attribute's
spelling is reproduced). A separate coordinate in the page is for a **camper
stop on Via Mustilli**, not the museum; do not use that coordinate for placement.

The route page says most rooms date to the Lombard period and describes their
clearance by property owner Nicola Fragnito during building-consolidation work;
finds were delivered to the Soprintendenza. It does not provide excavation-unit
numbers, dated floor sections, a plan, or a publication establishing phase dates.

The first room is described as a medieval public space containing an VIII-century
well serving several households. The minting bench, kitchen, weaving equipment,
scriptorium and arms displays are reenactment installations, not proof that each
room originally had that function. An Arechi II coin demonstration belongs to a
later eighth-century context, not the game year700.

The corridor is explicitly associated with the **legend** of Pope Benedict XIII
(Orsini), rebuilding after1688 and1702. The operator then says:

> “Originariamente si trattava di una strada, ovvero di un passaggio inerente
> alle fortificazioni”

That is a road or fortification-associated passage, near a defensive tower. It
does not tell us which dated phase provided a roof, how the road related to an
AD700 street surface, or that its full current extent existed in700. The same
page describes the tower's conversion to civilian building after the earthquakes.

The final, lower room is called a Lombard necropolis. This is a credible specific
lead for further archaeology, but “Lombard” spans several centuries locally.
Without the primary excavation record, do not assert use in700, a private-family
crypt, an extensive network, or the current staircase as an ancient access.

### Contemporary press corroboration and its limits

[NTR24,18 May2021](https://www.ntr24.tv/2021/05/18/triggio-pronto-il-living-museum-ntr24-alla-scoperta-della-benevento-sotterranea-dei-longobardi/)
describes the museum beside ancient walls, initially three levels/seven rooms,
with necropolis and “prison” areas planned for later opening. It explicitly calls
the secret passage **“di epoca successiva.”** This independently confirms the
visitor project, but the labels are not a stratigraphic publication. The current
operator page lists more rooms; that is not evidence of an ancient seven-room or
nine-room plan.

## 2. San Marco dei Sabariani

[NTR24,29 January2016](https://www.ntr24.tv/2016/01/29/la-petizione-dei-cittadini-salviamo-gli-affreschi-longobardi-della-cripta-di-san-marco-dei-sabariani/)
places the crypt in the eponymous square, reports discovery/excavation in2007,
and dates the paintings to the IX century. It reports a conservation petition,
not a primary excavation report. Painting date is not necessarily foundation
date, but also cannot be used to infer that the building already existed in700.

New evidence located in this pass:
[NTR24,3 April2025](https://www.ntr24.tv/2025/04/03/benevento-da-piazza-sabariani-riaffiorano-sepolture-romane-e-medioevali-ma-anche-una-domus-con-mosaici/).
Utility work in Piazza Sabariani was paused for archaeological investigation.
The body text tentatively reports at least three burials “di epoca romana e
precedenti il periodo medioevale,” and walls apparently defining a Roman domus
with mosaics/floors. The headline says Roman and medieval burials, while the body
uses different, tentative wording. Do not resolve that discrepancy by silently
assigning an exact date. No measured chamber dimensions or interconnected
galleries are supplied.

**Map consequence:** retain this as a localized archaeological/burial-site lead.
A tomb below a current square may originally have been a grave beneath an open
surface; it is not evidence of an accessible underground hall. An AD700 chamber,
original entrance, and roof/street relationship still require phased plans or a
published excavation report. The current press evidence does not justify moving
the game's catacomb network here.

## 3. Cathedral hypogeum and pseudocrypt

### Stronger institutional chronology

The [Diocese's cathedral history](https://www.diocesidibenevento.it/cattedrale/)
explicitly summarizes historical documents and excavation results:

1. Fifth-century paleochristian cathedral with atrium and quadriportico; the atrium
   subsequently became a burial area, the “paradiso,” for noble families and high
   prelates.
2. Architectural changes in VIII–IX centuries, specifically Bishop David and
   Prince Sico.
3. Enlargement, dismantling of the “paradiso,” facade and bell tower in XII–XIII.
4. Orsini restorations after1688/1702 and destruction in1943.

This is a primary institutional summary, stronger than tourist copy, although
it is not the excavation's detailed stratigraphic publication. It **does not
date the start of atrium burials precisely**. The basilica and atrium can underpin
the700 reconstruction; a particular grave arrangement at700 remains an inference.

The same page reports2005–2011 archaeological investigations and explicitly says
the archaeological route was inaugurated18 December2012 beneath the **new**
floor. [NTR24,10 November2009](https://www.ntr24.tv/2009/11/10/al-duomo-i-lavori-proseguono-spediti/)
describes construction of the floating floor and the visitor route below it.
This is particularly clear evidence against reading the museum itinerary as a
surviving ancient tunnel.

The [2013 diocesan announcement carried by NTR24](https://www.ntr24.tv/2013/01/07/duomo-di-benevento-dall8-gennaio-le-visite-guidate-al-percorso-ipogeo/)
locates the modern access beneath the presbytery at the end of the aisles to the
right of the main entrance. That is useful for identifying today's site, **not**
for reconstructing an early-medieval staircase.

[NTR24,28 November2015](https://www.ntr24.tv/2015/11/28/benevento-inaugurata-la-pseudocripta-del-museo-diocesano-mugione-la-storia-e-la-strada-verso-lumanita/)
describes the restored pseudocrypt reopening and modern exhibition narrative.
It does not establish that the displayed Roman/medieval/Gothic objects were all
in situ together or that the entire museum room configuration existed in700.

### Specific research trail for the missing section drawings

The Diocese's [“Il percorso ipogeo tra emergenze archeologiche e restauro”](https://www.diocesidibenevento.it/il-percorso-ipogeo-tra-emergenze-archeologiche-e-restauro/)
names archaeologists **Giannicola Barone and Rita Lucia Pisano** presenting the
excavation results, museum director **Mario Iadanza**, and architect **Riccardo
D'Uva** explaining the exhibition. It identifies institutional collaboration
with the Soprintendenza. These are concrete leads for published site plans and
levels; the event page itself does not contain those drawings. No contact was
made on the user's behalf.

## What remains unverified before precise underground reconstruction

For each proposed playable chamber, obtain a phased plan, a section with floor
and contemporary exterior-ground elevations, construction/use dating that
includes700, original access, and evidence for any claimed connection. Do not
subtract modern basement depth from the game's terrain and call that the ancient
level. A tomb, well shaft, former street, roofed crypt, and modern archaeological
walkway are distinct features.

Sources retrieved in full and local working extracts are retained under
`/workspace/npc-audit/research/underground/`. Searches on the working NTR24 and
Diocese public WordPress APIs led to the articles above. General search engines
returned challenges or unrelated results; those results were not used as
archaeological evidence. No archaeological publication establishing exact700
subterranean plans for these three sites was retrieved in this pass.

## Playable reconstruction authorized after this research

The owner explicitly requested a small underground Lombard burial site inspired
by the Living Museum and accepted plausible estimates. The implemented chamber
uses six slab-covered graves beneath an existing Triggio lot, with a street-side
stair and oil lamps. Its layout and depth are authored; the evidence requirements
above remain requirements for claiming an exact archaeological reconstruction,
not a block on this authorized game scene. No connected underground network is
claimed. See HISTORY.md and HANDOVER.md for placement and access. These distinctions
are kept in development notes, outside the player-facing reading cards.
