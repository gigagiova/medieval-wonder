# Furnishing analogues for the small synagogue and burial rooms

These are design references for the user-authorized plausible reconstruction in AD 700, not claims of excavated Beneventan plans. Research uncertainty belongs here; the user requested no uncertainty labels inside the game.

## Small synagogue

The official Archaeological Park of Ostia's [Sinagoga educational panel](https://ostiaantica.cultura.gov.it/pannelli-didattici-e-mappe/i-quartieri-costieri-e-le-domus-marittime/sinagoga/) was retrieved and read. It describes a building completely rebuilt in the fourth century: vestibule, worship hall, raised **bimah** for Torah reading directed toward Jerusalem, and a shrine storing Torah scrolls. An adjoining hall has benches along the walls; another room contains an oven for unleavened bread. This provides a regional late-antique functional analogue, not a template proving seventh-century Benevento's layout.

The same park's [Mensola della Sinagoga panel](https://ostiaantica.cultura.gov.it/pannelli-didattici-e-mappe/larea-centrale-e-i-complessi-di-rappresentanza/mensola-della-sinagoga/) describes the Torah cupboard's supporting architectural decoration: a **seven-branched menorah relief**, shofar, plant bundle and citron. A Greek inscription commemorates a donor's container for sacred scrolls.

For the small room, prioritize a clear prayer/reading space, modest raised reading area, protected scroll cupboard, wall benches and simple lamps. A seven-branched menorah relief is a more directly supported visual reference than a giant freestanding Temple-style candelabrum. Avoid importing modern pew rows, contemporary signage or a generic church altar. The exact cupboard, bench material, curtain and lighting arrangement are reconstruction choices; these details are not all specified by the panels. Do not reproduce Ostia's large colonnade simply to make the room look monumental.

## Lombard burial rooms

The Italia Langobardorum association's [Museo del Sannio page](https://longobardinitalia.it/museo-del-sannio/) was retrieved and read. It describes male and female funerary assemblages containing **coins, jewelry, weapons and other artifacts**. Its [Cividale archaeological museum page](https://longobardinitalia.it/scopri/cividale-del-friuli/museo-archeologico-nazionale/) independently identifies weapons, jewelry, coins and utensils from Lombard necropoleis as evidence of funerary ritual.

These sources support a few restrained grave goods. They do **not** establish a Beneventan system of extensive communal subterranean galleries. The requested explorable burial rooms should therefore read as modest reused/subterranean chambers containing individual graves, rather than a Roman-style catacomb network with stacked wall loculi presented as a standard Lombard practice. Individual simple stone-lined or slab-covered graves are design suggestions for the authorized reconstruction; their particular plan and placement here are not established by these museum pages. Avoid turning every grave into a treasure display or treating a furnished elite burial as representative of everyone.
