# San Modesto and San Pietro in the forum quarter — 28 October 2026

The owner identified two absent sites, San Modesto near modern Via Episcopio
and “San Pietro ad Forum.” These references must not be dismissed because an
older map brief omitted them. The available sources establish their historical
locations more securely than their precise state in AD 700. The owner has
explicitly authorized plausible earlier reconstructions; evidence grades remain
in development documentation, not in labels over the game world.

## San Modesto: old Triggio complex, not the modern parish

The [Italian Wikipedia article](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiesa_di_San_Modesto_(Benevento))
was retrieved successfully. Its text says the old church stood in Triggio and
was destroyed in September 1943; a new church in Rione Libertà then inherited
the dedication. It transmits a foundation **around 649 by Duda Parda**, with
relics donated by Gregory I to Arechis I and an abbot Bentogrado/Bentigrado.
This is a reported historical tradition, not a contemporary foundation charter
checked in this research.

The [parish's own history](https://sanmodestobn.wixsite.com/ilmiosito/storia),
also retrieved, instead says:

> “L' ex Chiesa di San Modesto si ergeva lungo Via Episcopio nel quartiere Triggio.”

It dates construction **758–774**, gives the first documentary appearance in
774 among Arechis's donations to Santa Sofia, and names **Leoniano** as founder.
It says a later upper entrance overlooked the slargo San Bartolomeo, today's
Piazza Orsini. [Sannio Report, 16 March 2015](https://www.sannioreport.com/2015/03/16/la-chiesa-ormai-ex-di-san-modesto/)
repeats this chronology and locates the ruins by Calata Olivella, close to the
Arco del Sacramento. These are not independent proof of two separate early
foundations; the conflicting chronology must remain explicit. The earlier
blanket `758+` exclusion in HISTORY also failed to acknowledge the 649 tradition.

### Geographic anchor

The Wikipedia infobox coordinate **41.1243083, 14.7706111 must not be used** for
the old church: it points south into the modern Rione Libertà parish area.
The narrative's historical location is a different place.

A freshly fetched OpenStreetMap extract identifies
[way 423501738](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/423501738), named
“Ruderi del chiostro dell'abbazia di San Modesto.” Its vertex-average coordinate
is **41.1304192 N, 14.7742414 E**. Bounds are approximately
41.1303160–41.1305008 N, 14.7740901–14.7745072 E. Via Episcopio passes immediately
north, matching the parish account. This is a **cloister-ruin anchor**, not a
survey of the seventh-century church footprint. Fit a modest church/precinct
to the registered game lot in this immediate area, recording the chosen lot
and displacement. Do not move it to modern Piazza San Modesto.

### AD 700 treatment

A small church/household religious foundation in this precinct is an authorized
**reconstruction hypothesis**, drawing on the 649 tradition while disclosing
the later documentary chronology. Do not represent the developed eighth-century
abbey, later cloister plan, scriptorial organization, or medieval privileges as
all established in 700. Use simple reused masonry, timber roofing, modest altar
and minimal ancillary accommodation; exact plan/orientation are inferred.

## “San Pietro ad Forum”: likely the San Pietro delle Monache precinct

The retrieved [Monastero di San Pietro delle Monache article](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastero_di_San_Pietro_delle_Monache)
locates the destroyed monastery **at the far end of modern Piazza Cardinal
Pacca**, with its church facade facing **west toward Via San Gennaro**. It
reused a Roman bath hall, preserving an opus-testaceum side wall and hypocaust.
The article's historical-ruin coordinate is **41.1317444 N, 14.7728028 E**.
This site is distinct from the already modeled **San Pietro extra muros**.

The article says it is securely documented from the **eleventh century**.
An earlier Lombard foundation is discussed, including a 1121 jurisdictional
claim invoking a privilege of Duke Liutprand, and a possible identification with
San Pietro de Duddi. Neither establishes an exact AD 700 building.

[Marcello Rotili, *Spazi monastici a Benevento* (2017), DOI
10.1484/J.HAM.5.113717, accessible text mirror](https://docslib.org/doc/12345608/spazi-monastici-a-benevento-marcello-rotili)
was retrieved. Its urban-map legend identifies:

> “In piazza cardinal Pacca, monastero di S. Pietro de monachabus sulle strutture
> di un edificio forse di tipo termale.”

This supports the **precinct identity/location**, not a date of 700. The mirror
exposes only part of the paper; this pass did not read every PDF page.

### Name caution and reconstruction

The exact name **“San Pietro ad Forum” was not verified** in the retrieved
sources. The Wikipedia article specifically says **two other churches** in
the square bore `de foro`; Rotili names **Santo Stefano de monialibus de Foro**
and **San Giacomo a Foro**. Thus “San Pietro in the forum quarter” is a useful
interpretation of the user's reference, but `ad Forum` must not be promoted as
an independently attested alias or a second separate church. Modern Via San
Pietro a Foro appears in industrial/commercial addresses and is not evidence
for moving this intra-mural precinct there.

For this reconstruction, add **one modest San Pietro precursor in the Pacca
precinct**, with reused Roman masonry and west-facing entry where the fixed
lot allows. Treat its AD 700 existence and precise dedication/plan as inference.
Do not duplicate it as both “ad Forum” and “delle Monache,” and do not import the
later two-cloister, city-largest monastery into 700. The adjacent Pacca burial
research is a related site layer, not proof of a connected citywide catacomb.
